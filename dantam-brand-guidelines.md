# Dantam Brand Guidelines

Use these guidelines when creating artwork, layouts, campaigns, social posts, ads, patient handouts, signage, presentations, website sections, or AI-generated creative material for Dantam Dental Care.

## Brand Snapshot

- Brand name: Dantam Dental Care
- Tagline: Smile for lifetime
- Category: Modern dental care clinic group
- Primary location context: Thane, Maharashtra, India, with associated clinics in Thane and Nalasopara
- Core promise: Honest, thoughtful, patient-first dentistry delivered with modern technology, warmth, and clinical excellence
- Founding doctors: Dr. Krushnakumar Modi and Dr. Blanch Gonsalves Modi
- Key services: Dental implants, single-sitting root canal treatments, aligners, braces, child dental care, tooth extraction, intraoral scanning, smile designing, teeth whitening, dentures, and full-mouth rehabilitation

## Brand Personality

Dantam should feel calm, trustworthy, modern, warm, and precise. The brand is not cold, corporate, flashy, luxury-for-luxury's-sake, or playful in a childish way.

Design should communicate:

- Clinical competence without intimidation
- Warmth without clutter
- Premium care without looking inaccessible
- Family-friendly confidence
- Digital dentistry and clean process
- Ethical, no-pressure guidance

Useful words: calm, honest, modern, gentle, precise, considered, warm, trusted, personal, family-first, long-term, confident.

Avoid words and visuals that feel: aggressive, discount-led, fear-based, overpromising, cosmetic-only, gimmicky, loud, chaotic, or overly hospital-like.

Logo usage:

- Use the full Dantam logo for most brand-facing artwork.
- Keep clear space around the logo equal to at least the height of the tooth-leaf mark.
- Place the logo on white, warm cream, very pale mint, or deep green backgrounds.
- On deep green backgrounds, use a version that remains high contrast. If only the standard logo is available, place it inside a white or cream holding area.
- Do not stretch, skew, rotate, crop, recolor, add shadows, add outlines, or place the logo over busy photos.
- Do not separate the tagline from the logo unless the composition clearly needs a standalone tagline.

### Color Ratios

Recommended composition:

- 45-60% warm background, cream, white, or soft mint
- 20-30% deep green
- 10-15% muted sage and forest text
- 5-8% accent green
- 2-5% gold

Gold is an accent, not a dominant background. Use it sparingly for premium cues, stars, small icons, section details, or primary CTA buttons.

### Approved Color Pairings

- Deep green text on warm background
- Deep green text on cream
- Forest text on white
- Muted sage body text on warm background or white
- Gold accents on deep green
- White text on deep green
- Accent green for links and small labels

Avoid:

- Large blocks of bright gold
- Neon dental blues
- Harsh clinical blue-and-white compositions
- Pure black as the dominant text color
- Red except for errors or urgent notices
- Overly beige layouts without enough deep green contrast

## Typography

The current website uses Google fonts:

- Headings: Cormorant Garamond
- Body and UI: Work Sans
- Signature/script accent: Dancing Script

### Font Roles

Use Cormorant Garamond for:

- Main headlines
- Section titles
- Doctor names
- Large stat numbers
- Elegant campaign headings

Use Work Sans for:

- Body copy
- Navigation
- Buttons
- Forms
- Captions
- Labels
- Service descriptions
- Legal or contact information

Use Dancing Script only for:

- The word or phrase that needs a human, emotional signature feel
- Tagline-style emphasis such as the website hero treatment: "Smile for lifetime"
- Small celebratory campaign moments

Do not use Dancing Script for long lines, paragraphs, service names, phone numbers, clinical instructions, or small text.

### Typography Scale

Use this as a practical design scale:

- Hero headline: 64-110 px desktop, 44-56 px mobile
- Page headline: 44-70 px desktop, 36-44 px mobile
- Section title: 34-52 px desktop, 30-38 px mobile
- Card title: 22-28 px
- Body: 16-18 px
- Small body/caption: 13-15 px
- Eyebrow label: 11-13 px, uppercase, bold, wide tracking
- Button text: 14-16 px, bold

### Typography Style

- Headings should feel elegant, spacious, and calm.
- Body copy should be clear, direct, and reassuring.
- Avoid negative letter spacing.
- Avoid all-caps headlines except small eyebrow labels.
- Avoid condensed fonts, futuristic fonts, cartoon fonts, distressed fonts, or decorative medical fonts.
- Keep line height generous for patient-facing copy.

## Layout And Composition

Dantam layouts should be clean, calm, and confidence-building. Use generous spacing, structured grids, and strong hierarchy.

Preferred patterns:

- Warm cream or pale mint hero backgrounds
- Deep green content bands for stats, CTAs, and footer-style sections
- White cards with pale borders for services, testimonials, locations, and forms
- Rounded corners that feel soft but not cartoonish
- Natural clinic and doctor photography as the main visual anchor
- Small gold accents for trust markers, stars, icons, and premium details

Recommended shape system:

- Buttons: pill shape
- Cards: softly rounded, around 16-18 px radius
- Image panels: larger radius, around 24-28 px
- Small badges: 12-18 px radius
- Dividers and borders: pale green-gray, thin, unobtrusive

Avoid:

- Crowded compositions
- Heavy drop shadows
- Overlapping text on busy photos
- Decorative dental clip art
- Tooth cartoons unless creating child-specific material
- Generic stock-photo smiles with no clinic relevance
- Excessive gradients or abstract blobs

## Iconography

Use simple outline icons with rounded geometry. The website uses Lucide-style icons, which are a good fit.

Preferred icon concepts:

- Calendar for booking
- Phone and WhatsApp for contact
- Star for reviews
- Shield check for sterilisation and safety
- Scan line for digital dentistry
- Award for experience
- Leaf for calm environment
- Sparkles for smile aesthetics
- Baby/child icon for pediatric dentistry
- Heart handshake for patient-first care

Icon color:

- Deep green or accent green for normal icons
- Gold for premium/trust icons
- White or gold icons on deep green backgrounds

Avoid filled, cartoonish, hyper-detailed, 3D, or mismatched icon sets.

## Copy Voice

Dantam copy should sound calm, specific, ethical, and reassuring.

Voice principles:

- Lead with patient benefit, then clinical credibility.
- Use simple, human language.
- Avoid pressure, scare tactics, or discount-led messaging.
- Explain modern technology as comfort and predictability, not as jargon.
- Mention doctors and experience where trust matters.
- Use "we" naturally, but keep focus on the patient.

Good phrases:

- "Modern dental care in one calm place."
- "Care that feels considered, not clinical."
- "Every treatment begins with a conversation, a clear plan, and an honest recommendation."
- "Book a friendly consultation and we will walk you through your options with zero pressure."
- "A calm, welcoming clinic designed to make dental visits feel restful, not clinical."
- "Complete dental care under one trusted team."

Avoid:

- "Cheapest dentist"
- "Guaranteed painless"
- "Perfect smile guaranteed"
- "Limited time miracle offer"
- "No. 1 dentist" unless a verified source is provided
- Fear-based copy such as "ignore this and lose your teeth"
- Overly cosmetic language that ignores health and function

## CTA Style

Primary calls to action:

- Book Appointment
- Book a visit
- Book Consultation
- WhatsApp us
- Call now
- Meet the team
- Read more stories

CTA hierarchy:

- Use gold buttons for the primary booking action on light backgrounds.
- Use deep green buttons for serious or high-trust actions.
- Use outline buttons for secondary actions like phone calls, WhatsApp, or supporting navigation.
- Do not use multiple competing primary CTAs in the same visual area.

## Social And Campaign Artwork

For social posts, ads, and event artwork:

- Use one strong message per creative.
- Keep Dantam Dental Care visibly present in the first viewport or top third.
- Include service context clearly: implants, aligners, pediatric dentistry, root canal, smile designing, etc.
- Use real doctor, clinic, or treatment imagery where possible.
- Put contact details in a clean footer or bottom band.
- Use gold sparingly for urgency, trust, dates, or campaign badges.
- Keep medical claims modest and specific.

Example structure:

- Top: logo or small brand lockup
- Main: one clear headline in Cormorant Garamond
- Middle: human image or clinic/service image
- Support: 2-3 concise benefit points in Work Sans
- Bottom: CTA, phone/WhatsApp, location

## Print And Patient Materials

For brochures, handouts, posters, in-clinic signage, and instructions:

- Prioritize readability over decoration.
- Use Work Sans for instruction-heavy material.
- Use deep green headings and forest text.
- Use cream or white backgrounds.
- Use pale mint blocks to group instructions.
- Use icons only when they improve scanning.
- Keep emergency or urgent notes visually distinct but not alarming unless medically necessary.

## Accessibility And Readability

- Maintain strong contrast, especially for older patients.
- Body text should usually be at least 16 px digitally and 10-11 pt in print.
- Do not place small text over photos.
- Avoid low-contrast gold text on cream.
- Avoid long paragraphs in narrow cards.
- Use clear section breaks, bullets, and short headings for patient instructions.

## Brand Do And Do Not

Do:

- Use deep green as the anchor color.
- Keep layouts warm, structured, and spacious.
- Use real Dantam assets whenever available.
- Pair elegant headings with clean sans-serif body text.
- Communicate safety, trust, comfort, and expertise.
- Make the clinic feel modern but personal.

Do not:

- Make the brand feel like a hospital chain.
- Use cold blue medical templates.
- Overuse tooth icons or dental cartoons.
- Make gold the dominant color.
- Use aggressive discounts as the main brand message.
- Use unrealistic smiles or heavily retouched teeth.
- Create fear-driven treatment ads.
- Mix too many typefaces.

## AI Creative Instructions

When using this file as a skill or AI prompt, follow these rules:

1. Create artwork that feels like a premium but approachable dental clinic in Thane.
2. Use the exact palette and typography roles above unless the output medium cannot support them.
3. Prefer authentic clinic, doctor, treatment, and family-care imagery over generic dental stock visuals.
4. Keep the tone ethical, warm, reassuring, and clinically credible.
5. Make "Dantam Dental Care" and "Smile for lifetime" available as brand identifiers, but do not force the tagline into every layout.
6. For patient-facing materials, prioritize clarity, comfort, and trust over visual drama.
7. For campaign materials, keep one main offer or message, one primary CTA, and visible contact/location details.
8. Avoid exaggerated medical claims, guaranteed outcomes, fear-based copy, or cosmetic overpromising.
